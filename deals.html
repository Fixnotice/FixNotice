How it's scored

Every signal is a public record. The score is just the ones that matter most, added up.

Vacant or unsecured (+4)The city ordered the structure secured, boarded or declared it vacant. The strongest sell signal there is.
Unsafe structure (+4)Unsafe, unfit for occupancy, order to vacate, demolition language. Repairs cost more than the owner wants to spend.
Open notices (+1 each, up to 6) and age (+1 to +2)Notices that have sat open for 3 to 6+ months mean an owner who isn't fixing it.
Housing court (+3)The city has taken the owner to court over the property.
Out-of-state owner (+3) or lives elsewhere (+2)From the tax roll's mailing address. Absentee owners sell; out-of-state owners sell by mail.
Lapsed rental registration (+1), multi-family (+1)A landlord who stopped registering is often done with the property.

Questions

How is this different from PropStream or a county list?

Those are built from county records that update monthly and don't include city code enforcement at all. This list is rebuilt every morning from the City of Buffalo's own violation feed, so a vacant-structure order is on the list the morning the city publishes it (about two weeks after the inspection, and usually before the owner has acted), with the inspector's words.

Can I contact the owners?

The owner's mailing address comes from the public tax roll, so a letter is the simplest route. Calls and texts have to follow the Do Not Call list and TCPA; that's on you, as with any list.

How many leads are there?

Roughly 150 to 250 properties score high enough at any time, with a few dozen new or changed each week. Quality over volume: the score puts the vacant, unsafe, out-of-state-owner properties at the top.

Do you cover other cities?
